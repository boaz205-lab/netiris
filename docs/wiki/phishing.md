# Understanding Smishing and Mobile Money Fraud

Mobile money is a vital part of daily life across Africa. However, its rapid adoption has made it a primary target for cybercriminals. According to INTERPOL's 2026 African Cyberthreat Assessment Report, generative AI tools are now enabling up to 55% of all cybercrime across the continent. A significant portion of this activity involves smishing (SMS phishing) and synthetic identity fraud specifically targeting mobile financial services.

## How These Scams Work
Criminals use AI to create highly convincing, personalized text messages that mimic official alerts from banks or mobile network operators. They often create a false sense of urgency, claiming your account is locked, a transaction failed, or you must verify your identity immediately.

### What to do: Protect your mobile money

**Never share your PIN or OTP:** Legitimate organizations will *never* ask for your Personal Identification Number (PIN) or One-Time Password (OTP) via SMS or phone call.
**Verify independently:** If you receive an urgent message, do not click any links. Open your mobile money app directly or call the official customer service number found on the back of your SIM card or the provider's official website.
**Report suspicious activity:** Forward suspicious SMS messages to your mobile network provider's fraud hotline and report the incident to your national CERT (e.g., KE-CIRT/CC in Kenya)
